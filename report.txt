2. SCENE LOGIC AND TIMING  (the most important finding)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Two different change rates run at the same time:

  MAJOR SCENE CHANGES (diff threshold 40)
    17 scenes across 61.48 s
    average hold .......... 3.63 s
    shortest .............. 0.50 s
    longest ............... 14.25 s

  MICRO EVENTS (diff threshold 10)
    98 events across 61.48 s
    average interval ...... 0.63 s

THE RULE:
  The SUBJECT changes slowly (~3.6 s per idea).
  The FRAME changes constantly (~every 0.6 s).

Within a single held scene, something is always arriving: a card slides in,
a stamp lands, a checkbox ticks, a number advances, confetti fires, an
annotation draws itself. The viewer never sees a static frame, but is also
never rushed through the message.

This is the opposite of the common mistake (changing topic quickly while each
topic sits still). Hold the idea; animate inside it.

Distribution of major scene holds:
    0.50 - 1.00 s   4 scenes   (punctuation / flash beats)
    1.00 - 3.00 s   5 scenes   (quick points)
    3.00 - 5.00 s   5 scenes   (normal explanation)
    7.00 s +        3 scenes   (the dense demonstration sections)

Long scenes are not static scenes. The 14.25 s scene contains roughly 22
micro events.

3. LAYOUT GRID  (identical in all 17 scenes)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  +--------------------------------------------------+
  |  // 15 · the entire workflow                     |   <- mono micro-label
  |                                                  |
  |  That's the entire                               |   <- line 1: bold sans
  |  workflow.                                       |   <- line 2: italic serif
  |                                                  |
  |                                                  |
  |        [  THE ONLY ZONE THAT VARIES  ]           |   <- the "stage"
  |        stamps / cards / screenshots /            |
  |        numerals / mockups / checklists           |
  |                                                  |
  |                                       ((char))   |   <- presenter, cropped
  +--------------------------------------------------+

Vertical zones (as a fraction of a 1280 px tall frame):

    0.00 - 0.06    dead margin
    0.07 - 0.09    monospace section label
    0.13 - 0.23    spoken caption (1-2 lines)
    0.28 - 0.72    THE STAGE  (all varying content lives here)
    0.68 - 1.00    presenter character, bottom-right, cropped by frame edge

Horizontal: a single generous left margin (~0.12 of width) that the label,
caption and most stage content all align to. The composition is left-aligned,
not centred — this is a major part of its editorial feel.

KEY STRUCTURAL CHOICES:
  * Captions are at the TOP, not the bottom.
  * The presenter is bottom-RIGHT and deliberately CROPPED by the frame edge.
  * Only one zone changes between scenes; the scaffolding never moves.

4. COLOUR PALETTE  (sampled directly from pixels)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    #ECEAE5     background     warm off-white / paper
    #0C0A08     ink            near-black (not pure black)
    #F83820     accent         red-orange
    #AEACA6     muted          monospace micro-labels
    #FBFAF7     card           slightly lighter than background
    #DCD9D2     line           card borders and inactive tracks

ONE accent colour only. It is reserved and always does the same jobs:
    - the emphasised word inside the headline
    - the stamp outline and half the stamp lettering
    - the active step pip
    - hand-drawn marks (underlines, circles, arrows)
    - the primary call-to-action

Everything else is paper, ink and grey. The restraint is what makes the red
read as meaningful rather than decorative.

The background is NOT pure white and the ink is NOT pure black. The slight
warmth on both is what makes it feel printed instead of digital.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. TYPOGRAPHY SYSTEM  (four roles, never more)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  ROLE 1 — HEADLINE / CAPTION
    Bold geometric sans, heavy weight (~800), tight negative letter-spacing.
    Colour: ink. Size: very large (~0.06 of frame height).
    Carries the spoken line.

  ROLE 2 — EMPHASIS
    Italic serif, slightly LARGER than the sans around it.
    Colour: accent red.
    Used on one or two key words INSIDE the headline, inline — not as a
    separate line. This mixed-face inline emphasis is the signature move.

  ROLE 3 — DISPLAY / STAMP
    Condensed sans caps, very heavy (Anton-like), tight tracking.
    Two-tone: part ink, part accent, split on the meaningful word.
    Always enclosed in a stamp box (see section 7).

  ROLE 4 — MICRO-LABEL
    Monospace, small, wide letter-spacing, grey.
    Always prefixed with "// ".
    Used for: section labels, card subtitles, status readouts.
    This single device does enormous work — it signals "technical",
    adds texture at small scale, and fills space without noise.

Hierarchy ratio is aggressive: the headline is roughly 3x the micro-label.
There is no mid-size text. Big or small, nothing in between.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. GRAPHICS TECHNIQUES  (the craft devices, in order of impact)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  7.1  PAPER GRAIN
       A fine monochrome noise texture over the ENTIRE frame, multiplied.
       Low opacity (~30%). Removes the flat digital look instantly.
       Cheapest high-impact device in the whole video.

  7.2  RUBBER-STAMP BADGE
       Condensed caps inside a thin red rectangular outline, rotated
       1-2 degrees off axis, with a distressed / eroded edge texture.
       Lands with a quick scale-down impact. Used once per scene as the
       "title" of the stage.

  7.3  MONOSPACE MICRO-LABELS
       "// claude code", "// steps done", "// 61% rendered".
       Placed under cards, beside numerals, above stamps.
       Adds density and a technical register at almost no visual cost.

  7.4  HAND-DRAWN RED MARKS
       Underlines, circles, arrows, brackets drawn in a rough marker style
       over screenshots and words. They APPEAR BY DRAWING THEMSELVES, left
       to right, rather than fading in.

  7.5  NUMBERED STEP BADGE + PROGRESS PIPS
       A black rounded square holding a large numeral, with a row of small
       pips beneath showing position in the sequence. Persistent across a
       multi-step section so the viewer always knows where they are.

  7.6  REAL SCREENSHOTS AS CONTENT
       Actual UI captures — not mockup illustrations — cropped, rounded,
       drop-shadowed and annotated. This is a large share of the credibility.

  7.7  CHECKLIST CARDS
       White rounded cards, each with a large black checkbox, a bold title
       and a monospace sub-note. Ticked progressively. Used for the summary.

  7.8  HALFTONE SHADING
       The illustrated character is shaded with visible halftone dots rather
       than smooth gradients, matching the printed-paper theme.

  7.9  CELEBRATORY PARTICLES
       Confetti and party poppers on the payoff beat only. Used once, so it
       still means something.

  7.10 DEVICE MOCKUPS
       Phone frames holding screenshots, sometimes two side by side for
       before/after comparison.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. ANIMATION LOGIC
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  ENTRANCES
    Elements arrive with a short overshoot (spring), not a linear fade.
    Typical duration 8-16 frames. Direction varies by element type:
      - cards and chips   : up from below
      - stamps            : scale down into place, slight rotation
      - annotations       : draw along their own path
      - numerals          : pop with scale
      - text              : reveal per word, in time with the voice

  STAGGER
    Grouped items (cards, checklist rows) never arrive together.
    Offset is 10-18 frames per item. This alone produces several of the
    "micro events" per second noted in section 2.

  THE PRESENTER
    Changes POSE between scenes (pointing, celebrating, shrugging,
    presenting) but barely moves within a scene. The pose is chosen to match
    the message. There is no continuous idle animation — the character is
    effectively a series of stills.

  CAMERA
    Completely locked. No zoom, no pan, no shake, at any point in 61 seconds.
    All energy comes from elements moving within a fixed frame.

  TRANSITIONS
    Hard cuts, plus occasional one-to-two-frame white flashes on the biggest
    changes. No dissolves, no slides between scenes.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. THE UNDERLYING RULES  (transferable to any topic)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

   1. Hold the idea ~3.5 s. Animate inside it every ~0.6 s.
   2. Fix the scaffolding (label, caption, character). Vary only the stage.
   3. Captions at the top. Presenter bottom-right, cropped.
   4. Left-align almost everything. Avoid centring.
   5. One accent colour, used only for meaning.
   6. Warm off-white background, warm near-black ink. Never pure values.
   7. Four type roles: heavy sans, italic serif accent, condensed display
      caps, monospace micro-label. Nothing in between in size.
   8. Put the emphasis word in italic serif INSIDE the sans headline.
   9. Grain over everything.
  10. Change the visual ARCHETYPE every scene, not just the content.
  11. Stagger every group. Nothing arrives simultaneously.
  12. Lock the camera. All motion is internal.
  13. Use real screenshots wherever the topic allows.
  14. Spend the celebration once, on the payoff.
  15. Keep a persistent step indicator through multi-step sections.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. WHAT IS HARD TO REPRODUCE WITHOUT NEW ASSETS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  These are design/asset problems, not code problems:

  A. A PRESENTER CHARACTER WITH MULTIPLE POSES
     The reference changes pose almost every scene. A single static
     character, however well drawn, loses a large share of the liveliness.
     Needs roughly 6 poses as transparent cut-outs:
       point / celebrate / shrug / present / think / approve

  B. A SCREENSHOT LIBRARY
     Much of the stage is annotated real UI. For any product video this
     means actually capturing the product's screens at the right moments.

  C. DISTRESSED STAMP TEXTURE
     The eroded edge on the stamp badge is a texture asset or a noise-based
     displacement filter, not a plain border.

  D. HALFTONE CHARACTER SHADING
     Must be baked into the character artwork, or applied as an SVG filter.

  Everything else in this document — layout, palette, typography, timing,
  stamps, cards, numerals, micro-labels, annotations, grain, stagger — is
  reproducible in code with no new artwork.
