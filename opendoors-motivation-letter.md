## 1. Description of scientific interests

My research interest is applied artificial intelligence, and specifically autonomous
agent systems that reason over private, domain-specific knowledge and then act on it
through real software tools. For five years I have built this class of system in
production rather than in a laboratory: retrieval-augmented pipelines over vector
stores, tool-calling and function-calling layers on top of large language models, and
orchestration graphs that keep long-running agents stable when a model's output is
probabilistic and the business process it drives is not.

Three questions interest me most. The first is reliability: how an agent detects and
recovers from its own failures — a malformed tool call, an invented field, an API that
times out — without a human intervening. The second is grounding: how chunking
strategy, embedding choice and retrieval ranking decide whether an answer is
trustworthy or merely fluent. The third is evaluation: how to measure an agentic
system when there is no single correct output to compare it against, which is the
problem I find hardest and least solved.

I want to move from engineering these systems to studying them properly — to
understand the mathematics and architecture beneath the interfaces I use every day,
and eventually to contribute to the design of agent frameworks rather than only
consuming them.

little of it.

My third goal is research output. I want to publish work, not only ship products — to
contribute to how these systems are understood, not only to how many of them exist.

## 4. The reason for choosing a subject

I chose this subject because I reached its open problems from the wrong direction and
would like to correct that.

Every agentic system I have deployed eventually fails in a way I cannot fully explain.
A retrieval step returns context that is plausible and wrong. An agent loops. A tool
call succeeds while the reasoning that produced it was mistaken. I can patch these
symptoms with engineering, but I cannot yet reason about them from first principles,
because I have never formally studied what sits underneath — the statistics of
embedding spaces, the behaviour of models under distribution shift, the formal
properties of planning and search.

There is also a reason closer to how I think about building things. I am reading Don
Norman's work on design at the moment, and his argument that good design becomes
invisible describes exactly what I believe an automated system should achieve: an
automation that truly works is one its user stops noticing. Making a system that quiet
requires understanding it far more deeply than making one that merely runs.

I want this subject not as a credential but as the vocabulary for problems I already
have in front of me.

## 5. The reason for choosing to study in Russia

I want to study in Russia for a specific reason rather than a general one: its
mathematical and engineering tradition is strongest exactly where I am weakest.

My profile is unbalanced. I have five years of applied practice and comparatively
little theory — I can build a retrieval pipeline, but I cannot derive why one
embedding geometry behaves better than another. Russian higher education is known for
the opposite emphasis: rigorous, fundamentals-first instruction in mathematics,
algorithms and computer science, taught as a discipline rather than as a set of tools.
That is the inverse of my background and precisely what I need. Russian universities
also maintain strong research groups in artificial intelligence, data science and
systems engineering, where a master's student is expected to contribute to research
rather than only attend courses.

The Open Doors olympiad is itself part of the reason. It selects candidates on
demonstrated ability rather than on the ability to pay, which for someone from
Gilgit-Baltistan is not a small consideration.

Finally, Pakistan and Russia are expanding their cooperation in technology and
education, and I would like to work at that intersection — trained in Russia, then
building and teaching in Pakistan.
